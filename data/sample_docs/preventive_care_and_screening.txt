PREVENTIVE CARE AND HEALTH SCREENING

Source: Educational content based on USPSTF (US Preventive Services Task Force), CDC, and NHS guidelines

OVERVIEW
Preventive healthcare focuses on disease prevention, early detection, and health promotion rather than treating symptoms only after clinical disease has developed. Prevention is traditionally classified into three levels:
1. Primary Prevention: Preventing disease before it occurs through immunization, smoking cessation, healthy nutrition, and regular exercise.
2. Secondary Prevention: Identifying disease in its early, asymptomatic stages through screening tests so that treatment can be initiated promptly to halt progression (e.g., mammograms, colonoscopy, blood pressure checks).
3. Tertiary Prevention: Managing established chronic diseases to prevent complications, disability, and loss of functional independence (e.g., glycemic control in diabetes, cardiac rehabilitation).

AGE-BASED SCREENING GUIDELINES FOR ADULTS

1. Cardiovascular and Metabolic Health:
- Blood Pressure: Screening every 1 to 2 years for adults aged 18 to 39 with normal blood pressure (< 120/80 mmHg), and annually for adults aged 40 and older or those with risk factors (overweight, family history).
- Lipid Profile (Cholesterol): Fasting or non-fasting lipid screening starting between ages 20 and 35 for men and 20 and 45 for women, repeated every 4 to 6 years in average-risk individuals to evaluate cardiovascular risk.
- Type 2 Diabetes Screening: Fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c, or oral glucose tolerance test starting at age 35 for all adults who are overweight or obese, repeated at least every 3 years. Earlier screening is indicated for those with high-risk ethnic backgrounds or gestational diabetes history.

2. Cancer Screenings:
- Colorectal Cancer Screening: Recommended for all average-risk adults aged 45 to 75 years. Screening options include colonoscopy every 10 years, annual fecal immunochemical test (FIT), or stool DNA-FIT test every 3 years. Early detection allows removal of pre-cancerous polyps.
- Breast Cancer Screening: Biennial screening mammography is recommended for women aged 40 to 74 years to detect early-stage breast lesions before palpable masses develop.
- Cervical Cancer Screening: Cervical cytology (Pap smear) alone every 3 years for women aged 21 to 29 years; for women aged 30 to 65, options include high-risk HPV testing every 5 years, HPV plus cytology co-testing every 5 years, or cytology alone every 3 years.
- Lung Cancer Screening: Annual low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) for adults aged 50 to 80 years who have a 20 pack-year smoking history and currently smoke or have quit within the past 15 years.
- Prostate Cancer Screening: Shared decision-making regarding PSA (prostate-specific antigen) testing for men aged 55 to 69 years.

3. Bone Health:
- Osteoporosis Screening: Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) bone density screening is recommended for all women aged 65 and older, and for younger postmenopausal women with significant clinical fracture risk factors.

4. Skin Cancer Awareness:
Perform periodic skin self-examinations using the ABCDE rule for assessing pigmented skin lesions (moles):
- A - Asymmetry: One half does not match the other.
- B - Border irregularity: Edges are ragged, notched, or blurred.
- C - Color variation: Uneven shades of brown, black, red, white, or blue.
- D - Diameter: Greater than 6 mm (about the size of a pencil eraser).
- E - Evolving: Any change in size, shape, elevation, bleeding, itching, or crusting over time.

5. General Cancer Warning Signs (The CAUTION Acronym):
- C: Change in bowel or bladder habits
- A: A sore that does not heal
- U: Unusual bleeding or discharge
- T: Thickening or lump in breast, testicle, or elsewhere
- I: Indigestion or difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
- O: Obvious change in a wart or mole
- N: Nagging cough or persistent hoarseness
- Unexplained weight loss, drenching night sweats, or persistent fever

ADULT IMMUNIZATION RECOMMENDATIONS
- Influenza Vaccine: Annual vaccination for all individuals aged 6 months and older.
- COVID-19 Vaccine: Periodic boosters according to updated public health guidelines.
- Tetanus, Diphtheria, Pertussis (Tdap): Tdap booster once in adulthood, followed by Td or Tdap booster every 10 years, and during each pregnancy.
- Shingles (Herpes Zoster) Vaccine: Two doses of recombinant zoster vaccine (Shingrix) for adults aged 50 and older to prevent shingles and post-herpetic neuralgia.
- Pneumococcal Vaccine: Recommended for adults aged 65 and older, and younger adults with chronic medical conditions (asthma, COPD, diabetes, heart disease).
