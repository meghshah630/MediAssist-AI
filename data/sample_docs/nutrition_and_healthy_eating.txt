NUTRITION AND HEALTHY EATING

Source: Educational content based on WHO, NHS, and USDA Dietary Guidelines

OVERVIEW
Nutrition is fundamental to health, growth, disease prevention, and vitality across all life stages. A balanced dietary pattern provides necessary macronutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats) and micronutrients (vitamins and minerals), along with dietary fiber and water. Poor nutrition contributes to major global health burdens including cardiovascular diseases, type 2 diabetes, obesity, anemia, and certain cancers.

MACRONUTRIENT PRINCIPLES
1. Carbohydrates: The body's primary energy source, particularly for the central nervous system and exercising muscles.
   - Complex Carbohydrates: Whole grains (oats, brown rice, quinoa, whole wheat), legumes, vegetables, and tubers provide sustained energy, vitamins, and minerals.
   - Dietary Fiber: Recommended intake is 25 to 38 grams per day for adults. Soluble fiber (found in oats, beans, apples) lowers LDL cholesterol and stabilizes blood glucose. Insoluble fiber (whole wheat bran, vegetables) promotes regular bowel movements and gut health.
   - Free Sugars: The World Health Organization recommends limiting free and added sugars to less than 10% of total daily energy intake (approximately 50 grams for a 2000 kcal diet), with additional health benefits when kept under 5% (less than 25 grams).

2. Dietary Proteins: Essential for muscle synthesis, tissue repair, immune function, hormones, and enzymes.
   - Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA): 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day for sedentary adults, higher (1.2 to 2.0 g/kg) for older adults, athletes, and during recovery.
   - High-Quality Sources: Fish, poultry, eggs, dairy products (milk, yogurt, cottage cheese), legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans), soy products (tofu, tempeh), nuts, and seeds. Plant-based diets benefit from consuming diverse protein sources to ensure all essential amino acids are provided.

3. Dietary Fats: Vital for cell membrane structure, hormone synthesis, brain function, and absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K).
   - Unsaturated Fats (Healthy): Monounsaturated fats (extra virgin olive oil, avocados, almonds) and polyunsaturated fats, including essential Omega-3 fatty acids (salmon, mackerel, sardines, flaxseeds, walnuts) and Omega-6 fatty acids.
   - Saturated Fats: Limit to less than 10% of total energy intake; found in fatty cuts of meat, butter, palm oil, and full-fat dairy products.
   - Trans Fats: Industrially produced partially hydrogenated oils should be avoided entirely due to strong associations with coronary heart disease.

ESSENTIAL MICRONUTRIENTS
- Iron: Vital for hemoglobin synthesis and oxygen transport. Found as heme iron in red meat, poultry, and fish, and non-heme iron in lentils, spinach, beans, and fortified grains. Non-heme iron absorption is enhanced by vitamin C and inhibited by phytates, tea, and calcium.
- Calcium: Critical for bone mineral density, nerve transmission, and muscle contraction. Found in dairy, calcium-fortified plant milks, tofu, and leafy greens.
- Vitamin D: Supports calcium absorption and immune function. Produced endogenously through skin exposure to sunlight; dietary sources include oily fish and fortified foods. Supplementation is often recommended during winter months or in low-sunlight regions.
- Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin): Required for red blood cell maturation and neurological integrity. Found naturally in animal products; vegetarians and vegans require fortified foods or regular B12 supplements.
- Folate (Vitamin B9): Essential for DNA synthesis and prevention of neural tube defects during fetal development. Found in dark leafy greens, legumes, and fortified grain products.
- Potassium: Counters the hypertensive effects of sodium and aids cardiovascular function. Found in bananas, potatoes, oranges, spinach, and tomatoes.
- Sodium Limit: Limit sodium intake to under 2000 mg per day (equivalent to approximately 5 grams of table salt) to help control arterial blood pressure.

HEALTHY DIETARY PATTERNS
The Mediterranean Diet, DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension), and plant-forward dietary patterns consistently demonstrate the strongest clinical evidence for long-term health:
- Make half the plate vegetables and fruits with a variety of colors.
- Make one quarter of the plate whole grains.
- Make one quarter of the plate lean, protein-rich foods.
- Choose healthy plant oils over butter or lard.
- Drink predominantly water, unflavoured tea, or black coffee rather than sugar-sweetened beverages.
- Limit intake of ultra-processed packaged snacks, processed meats (sausages, bacon, hot dogs), and excessive alcohol.
