COVID-19 – SYMPTOMS, VARIANTS, VACCINATION, AND LONG COVID

OVERVIEW
COVID-19 (Coronavirus Disease 2019) is an infectious disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 (Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2) virus. It was declared a pandemic by the World Health Organization (WHO) in March 2020. The virus spreads primarily through respiratory droplets and aerosols from infected individuals, as well as via contaminated surfaces (fomite transmission).

VIROLOGY AND TRANSMISSION
SARS-CoV-2 is a single-stranded, positive-sense RNA beta-coronavirus. Its spike protein binds to the ACE2 (angiotensin-converting enzyme 2) receptor on human cells to gain entry. The incubation period ranges from 2 to 14 days (typically 4–6 days). Transmission occurs via:
- Close contact with infected persons (within 2 metres / 6 feet)
- Inhalation of respiratory aerosols in poorly ventilated indoor spaces
- Contact with contaminated surfaces followed by touching the face
- Highest viral shedding occurs in the 2 days before and 5 days after symptom onset

SYMPTOMS
COVID-19 presents with a wide spectrum of severity:

Mild to Moderate Symptoms:
- Fever or chills
- Cough (dry or productive)
- Fatigue and malaise
- Shortness of breath or difficulty breathing
- Loss of taste (ageusia) or smell (anosmia) – characteristic of earlier variants
- Sore throat and runny nose
- Muscle aches and headache
- Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhoea
- Skin rashes ("COVID toes" – chilblain-like lesions)

Severe Symptoms (requiring emergency care):
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Severe shortness of breath (oxygen saturation < 94%)
- New confusion or altered consciousness
- Cyanosis (bluish discolouration of lips or fingertips)
- Inability to remain awake

MAJOR VARIANTS
- Alpha (B.1.1.7): First identified UK; 50–70% more transmissible
- Delta (B.1.617.2): Dominant 2021; high transmissibility, more severe disease
- Omicron (B.1.1.529) and sub-lineages (BA.1–5, XBB, BQ.1, EG.5, JN.1): Highly mutated spike protein; increased immune evasion; generally milder disease but explosive spread. Later Omicron sub-variants (XBB.1.5, EG.5, JN.1) showed further immune evasion.

Risk factors for severe disease:
- Older age (> 60 years, risk increases dramatically with age)
- Immunocompromised status (HIV, immunosuppressive therapy, haematological malignancies)
- Obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²)
- Diabetes mellitus (particularly poorly controlled)
- Cardiovascular disease and hypertension
- Chronic kidney or liver disease
- Chronic respiratory disease (COPD, moderate-to-severe asthma)
- Cancer (especially blood cancers)
- Pregnancy

DIAGNOSIS
- RT-PCR (reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction) test: Gold standard; high sensitivity and specificity; detects viral RNA from nasopharyngeal swab, throat swab, or saliva sample.
- Rapid antigen tests (lateral flow devices): Useful for symptomatic individuals and mass screening; lower sensitivity than PCR; highly specific. Most reliable during peak infectivity period.
- Serology (antibody tests): Detect past infection or vaccination response.
- Chest CT: Shows ground-glass opacities, consolidation in moderate-severe disease.
- Chest X-ray: May show bilateral infiltrates in pneumonia.

TREATMENT
Mild to Moderate COVID-19:
- Supportive care: Rest, adequate hydration, antipyretics (paracetamol/acetaminophen) for fever
- Antiviral therapy (for high-risk patients within 5 days of symptom onset):
  * Nirmatrelvir-ritonavir (Paxlovid): Oral protease inhibitor; significantly reduces hospitalisation/death; multiple drug interactions
  * Remdesivir: IV antiviral; approved for hospitalised patients
  * Molnupiravir: Oral antiviral; modest efficacy; not recommended in pregnancy

Severe/Critical COVID-19:
- Dexamethasone 6 mg daily (10 days): Reduces mortality in patients requiring supplemental oxygen
- Baricitinib or Tocilizumab: IL-6 or JAK inhibitor; for patients with rapidly increasing oxygen requirements and elevated inflammatory markers
- Supplemental oxygen: Target SpO2 ≥ 94%; high-flow nasal oxygen, CPAP/NIV for hypoxaemic failure; mechanical ventilation in ARDS
- Therapeutic anticoagulation: For hospitalised patients to prevent thromboembolic complications
- Prone positioning: For mechanically ventilated patients with ARDS

VACCINATION
mRNA vaccines (Pfizer-BioNTech BNT162b2, Moderna mRNA-1273):
- Two-dose primary series plus boosters
- Highly effective at preventing severe disease, hospitalisation, and death
- Updated bivalent and XBB.1.5-targeting boosters available for broad protection
- Common side effects: Injection site pain, fatigue, headache, myalgia, fever (typically 1–2 days)
- Rare: Myocarditis (especially in young males after second mRNA dose; usually mild and self-resolving)

Adenoviral vector vaccines (Oxford-AstraZeneca ChAdOx1, Johnson & Johnson):
- Single or two-dose regimens
- Rare: Vaccine-induced immune thrombocytopenia and thrombosis (VITT)

Protein subunit vaccines (Novavax NVX-CoV2373): For those who prefer non-mRNA technology.

LONG COVID (Post-Acute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 Infection)
Definition: New, returning, or ongoing symptoms persisting more than 12 weeks after acute infection.
Prevalence: Estimated 10–30% of non-hospitalised cases; higher in those hospitalised.

Common symptoms:
- Persistent fatigue (most common)
- Post-exertional malaise (worsening after physical/mental activity)
- Brain fog (cognitive impairment, difficulty concentrating)
- Breathlessness and chest pain
- Heart palpitations and postural tachycardia (POTS)
- Sleep disturbances
- Anxiety and depression
- Joint and muscle pain
- Hair loss (telogen effluvium)
- Anosmia or parosmia (distorted smell)

Management of Long COVID:
- Multidisciplinary approach (respiratory, cardiology, physiotherapy, psychology)
- Pacing activity to avoid post-exertional malaise
- Cognitive rehabilitation for brain fog
- Physiotherapy (breathing exercises, graded activity)
- Pharmacological: Treat specific symptoms (antihistamines for mast cell activation, low-dose naltrexone being studied)
- No approved specific treatments; clinical trials ongoing

PREVENTION MEASURES
- Vaccination (most effective)
- Ventilation improvement in indoor spaces
- Wearing FFP2/N95 masks in high-risk settings
- Hand hygiene
- Isolation of cases; test-and-trace strategies
