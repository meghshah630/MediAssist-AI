WEIGHT MANAGEMENT AND OBESITY

Source: Educational content based on CDC, WHO, and NHS guidelines

OVERVIEW
Healthy weight management involves achieving and maintaining a body weight that supports overall health and minimizes the risk of chronic diseases. Body Mass Index (BMI) is the standard screening tool used to categorize body weight relative to height. BMI is calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m2).

BMI CLASSIFICATION (WHO Standards for Adults):
- Underweight: BMI less than 18.5 kg/m2
- Normal weight: BMI 18.5 to 24.9 kg/m2
- Overweight: BMI 25.0 to 29.9 kg/m2
- Class 1 Obesity: BMI 30.0 to 34.9 kg/m2
- Class 2 Obesity: BMI 35.0 to 39.9 kg/m2
- Class 3 Obesity (Severe/Morbid): BMI 40.0 kg/m2 or higher

Waist circumference is an additional clinical measurement assessing abdominal visceral fat. A waist circumference greater than 94 cm (37 inches) in men or 80 cm (31.5 inches) in women indicates increased health risks.

HEALTH RISKS OF OVERWEIGHT AND OBESITY
Excess body weight and visceral adiposity increase the risk of numerous medical conditions:
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus and insulin resistance
- Cardiovascular diseases including coronary artery disease, heart attack, and stroke
- Hypertension (high blood pressure)
- Dyslipidemia (elevated triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol)
- Obstructive sleep apnea and hypoventilation syndromes
- Osteoarthritis, particularly in weight-bearing joints (knees, hips, lower back)
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)
- Gallbladder disease
- Certain cancers (colorectal, breast in postmenopausal women, endometrial, kidney, esophageal)
- Psychological conditions including depression, anxiety, and reduced quality of life

EVIDENCE-BASED PRINCIPLES OF WEIGHT LOSS
Sustainable weight management is based on establishing a safe, consistent energy deficit combined with behavioral modifications:
1. Calorie Deficit: A deficit of 500 to 750 kcal per day below estimated total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) typically results in a safe rate of weight loss of approximately 0.5 to 1.0 kg (1 to 2 pounds) per week.
2. Realistic Goals: Losing 5% to 10% of baseline body weight over 6 months significantly improves glycemic control, blood pressure, lipid profile, and overall cardiovascular risk.
3. Gradual Approach: Rapid crash diets are generally unsustainable, promote lean muscle mass loss, reduce basal metabolic rate, and lead to rebound weight regain (yo-yo dieting).

DIETARY STRATEGIES FOR WEIGHT MANAGEMENT
- Dietary Patterns: Emphasize nutrient-dense, minimally processed foods such as vegetables, whole fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, nuts, and seeds. The Mediterranean and DASH dietary patterns offer strong evidence for weight control and cardiometabolic health.
- Protein Intake: Adequate dietary protein (1.2 to 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight) supports satiety, preserves lean muscle tissue during caloric restriction, and increases diet-induced thermogenesis.
- Dietary Fiber: Consuming 25 to 35 grams of fiber daily from vegetables, beans, oats, and whole grains slows gastric emptying, stabilizes postprandial glucose, and promotes fullness.
- Ultra-Processed Foods and Liquid Calories: Minimize intake of sugar-sweetened beverages, refined carbohydrates, commercial pastries, and energy-dense fast foods.
- Hydration: Drinking water before meals aids satiety and replaces high-calorie sugary beverages.

PHYSICAL ACTIVITY GUIDELINES
- Aerobic Exercise: Aim for at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity (such as brisk walking, cycling, or swimming) per week, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity exercise.
- Resistance Training: Perform muscle-strengthening exercises involving major muscle groups at least 2 days per week to preserve metabolic rate and functional strength during weight loss.
- Reducing Sedentary Time: Break up prolonged sitting with light activity every 30 to 60 minutes.

BEHAVIORAL AND LIFESTYLE FACTORS
- Sleep Duration: Chronic sleep restriction (less than 7 hours per night) disrupts appetite-regulating hormones, elevating ghrelin (hunger hormone) and lowering leptin (satiety hormone), driving food cravings.
- Stress Management: Chronic elevated cortisol promotes central abdominal fat deposition and emotional eating. Mindful eating practices and stress reduction are important.
- Self-Monitoring: Regular tracking of dietary intake, weekly body weight measurements, and physical activity logs consistently correlate with long-term weight maintenance.

MEDICAL AND CLINICAL INTERVENTIONS
When lifestyle modifications alone are insufficient for individuals with significant obesity or obesity-related comorbidities:
- Pharmacotherapy: Medications such as GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide, liraglutide) and orlistat may be prescribed under medical supervision for adults with BMI 30 kg/m2 or higher, or BMI 27 kg/m2 or higher with weight-related comorbidities. Note: Blood pressure medications (such as ACE inhibitors or beta-blockers) are treatments for hypertension, not weight-loss agents.
- Bariatric Surgery: Metabolic and bariatric surgery (e.g., sleeve gastrectomy, gastric bypass) is an established option for patients with BMI 40 kg/m2 or higher, or BMI 35 kg/m2 or higher with serious comorbidities who have not achieved sustainable loss with non-surgical interventions.
