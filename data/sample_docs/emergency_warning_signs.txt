MEDICAL EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS AND RED FLAGS

Source: Educational content based on American Heart Association (AHA), CDC, and NHS guidelines

OVERVIEW
Recognizing critical medical warning signs and initiating prompt emergency care saves lives and prevents irreversible organ damage or death. When encountering any red-flag emergency symptoms, immediately contact local emergency medical services (such as 999 in the UK, 911 in the USA/Canada, 112 in Europe, or 000 in Australia). Do not attempt to drive yourself to the emergency department if experiencing symptoms of a heart attack, stroke, or severe respiratory distress.

CRITICAL MEDICAL EMERGENCIES AND RECOGNITION

1. Acute Coronary Syndrome (Heart Attack / Myocardial Infarction):
Caused by acute blockage of blood flow to the heart muscle.
- Classic Warning Signs:
  * Persistent pressure, tightness, squeezing, fullness, or severe aching pain in the center of the chest lasting more than a few minutes, or resolving and returning.
  * Radiation of pain to one or both arms (especially the left arm), neck, jaw, back, or epigastrium (upper abdomen).
  * Shortness of breath (dyspnea) with or without chest discomfort.
  * Cold sweat, profuse diaphoresis, sudden dizziness, lightheadedness, or unexplained nausea/vomiting.
- Atypical Presentation in Women, Elderly, and Diabetic Patients: Often present without classic crushing chest pain, experiencing predominantly severe fatigue, shortness of breath, nausea, back pain, or indigestion-like discomfort.
- Immediate Action: Call 999/911. Keep the patient resting comfortably. If not allergic and no active bleeding, an adult dose of aspirin (300 mg chewed, not swallowed whole) may be recommended while awaiting the ambulance.

2. Acute Ischemic or Hemorrhagic Stroke (Brain Attack):
Rapid loss of brain function due to interrupted blood supply or intracranial hemorrhage.
- The FAST Assessment:
  * F - Facial Drooping: Ask the person to smile. Does one side of the face droop or feel numb?
  * A - Arm Weakness: Ask the person to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward or feel weak/numb?
  * S - Speech Difficulty: Ask the person to repeat a simple sentence. Is speech slurred, garbled, or are they unable to speak/understand?
  * T - Time to Call Emergency Services: If any one of these signs is present, call 999/911 immediately. Note the exact time symptoms were last known normal (critical for thrombolysis / clot-busting eligibility within a 4.5-hour window).
- Other Stroke Signs: Sudden severe "thunderclap" headache (worst headache of life, suggestive of subarachnoid hemorrhage), sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes, sudden trouble walking, loss of balance, or dizziness.

3. Anaphylaxis (Severe Life-Threatening Allergic Reaction):
Rapid-onset systemic hypersensitivity reaction affecting airway, breathing, and circulation.
- Warning Signs:
  * Swelling of the lips, tongue, uvula, or throat (angioedema) causing difficulty swallowing or hoarse voice.
  * Severe respiratory distress: Wheezing, persistent cough, or stridor (high-pitched whistling sound during inhalation).
  * Skin manifestations: Generalized hives (urticaria), intense itching, flushing, or pale/clammy skin.
  * Circulatory collapse: Dizziness, faintness, sudden profound hypotension, loss of consciousness.
- Immediate Action: Administer Intramuscular Adrenaline / Epinephrine auto-injector (e.g., EpiPen 0.3 mg for adults) into the outer mid-thigh immediately. Call 999/911. Lay the patient flat with legs elevated (unless breathing difficulty requires sitting upright). A second dose may be administered after 5 to 10 minutes if symptoms do not improve.

4. Sepsis (Life-Threatening Infection Response):
The body's dysregulated response to an infection, causing widespread tissue damage and organ failure.
- Red Flags:
  * S - Slurred speech or acute confusion
  * E - Extreme shivering or severe muscle pain
  * P - Passing no urine in 18 to 24 hours
  * S - Severe breathlessness (respiratory rate > 25/min)
  * I - "It feels like I am going to die" / extreme subjective illness
  * S - Skin mottled, pale, or bluish discolouration; non-blanching petechial rash (meningococcal disease)
- The Golden Hour: Early recognition, intravenous fluid resuscitation, and administration of broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour dramatically reduce sepsis mortality.

5. Diabetic Emergencies:
- Severe Hypoglycemia (Blood glucose < 3.0 mmol/L or 54 mg/dL): Profuse sweating, trembling, confusion, combativeness, blurred vision, seizures, or coma. If conscious, administer 15-20 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate (fruit juice, glucose tablets). If unconscious, place in recovery position and call 999/911; do not force food or liquids into the mouth.
- Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) / Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS): High blood sugar accompanied by persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity-smelling acetone breath, rapid deep Kussmaul breathing, extreme dehydration, and confusion. Requires immediate emergency hospital admission.
