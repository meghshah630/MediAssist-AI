GENERAL FIRST AID – CPR, BURNS, CHOKING, BLEEDING, AND FRACTURES

OVERVIEW
First aid is the immediate assistance given to any person suffering a sudden illness or injury before professional emergency medical care arrives. Effective first aid can save lives, prevent conditions from worsening, and promote recovery. The fundamental principles of first aid are summarised by the acronym DR ABC: Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing, Circulation.

CALLING FOR EMERGENCY HELP
Always call emergency services first in a life-threatening situation:
- UK: 999 (Emergency) or 111 (Non-emergency medical advice)
- USA/Canada: 911
- EU: 112
- Australia: 000

When calling, provide:
1. Your exact location (address, landmarks)
2. Nature of the emergency and number of casualties
3. What has already been done (first aid given)
4. Your name and callback number
Do NOT hang up until the dispatcher tells you to.

CARDIOPULMONARY RESUSCITATION (CPR)

Basic Life Support (BLS) for Adults – Chain of Survival:
1. Recognition of cardiac arrest and call for help (999/911)
2. Early CPR with emphasis on chest compressions
3. Rapid defibrillation (AED)
4. Advanced life support
5. Post-resuscitation care

CPR Technique (Adult):
Step 1: Ensure scene safety; check for danger
Step 2: Check for response: tap shoulders firmly and call out "Are you OK?"
Step 3: If unresponsive: Shout for help; call 999 or send someone to call
Step 4: Open airway: Head-tilt chin-lift; look in mouth for obvious obstruction
Step 5: Check for breathing: Look, listen, feel for no more than 10 seconds
Step 6: If not breathing normally: Start CPR immediately
  - Chest compressions: Place heel of hand on centre of chest (lower half of sternum)
  - Interlock fingers; keep arms straight; compress 5–6 cm depth at rate of 100–120/minute
  - Allow full chest recoil between compressions; minimise interruptions
  - 30 compressions : 2 rescue breaths (30:2 ratio) – if trained and willing
  - Compression-only CPR is acceptable and preferred by untrained bystanders
Step 7: Continue until AED available, casualty recovers, or professional help arrives

Defibrillation (AED):
- Switch on AED, follow audio/visual prompts
- Attach pads: one below right collarbone, one below left armpit
- Stand clear during rhythm analysis and shock delivery
- Resume CPR immediately after shock (minimise interruption)

CPR for Children (1–8 years): 30:2 ratio; compress by 1/3 chest depth; use one or two hands
Infant CPR (under 1 year): 30:2; two-finger compressions on lower sternum; compress by 4 cm; tilts chin gently

Recovery Position:
For unconscious casualty who is breathing: Place on side (recovery/lateral) position to maintain airway and prevent aspiration. Tilt head back slightly to keep airway open.

CHOKING

Mild Choking (can cough, speak, breathe):
- Encourage to continue coughing vigorously
- Do NOT perform back blows until coughing ineffective

Severe Choking (cannot cough, speak, or breathe; clutching throat):
Step 1: Call 999/911 or have someone call
Step 2: Give up to 5 back blows
  - Stand to the side and slightly behind casualty
  - Support chest with one hand; lean casualty forward
  - Give firm blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand
Step 3: Give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre)
  - Stand behind casualty; make a fist
  - Place fist just above navel, thumb side in
  - Grasp with other hand; pull sharply inward and upward
Step 4: Alternate 5 back blows with 5 abdominal thrusts until object dislodged or casualty loses consciousness
Step 5: If unconscious: Lower to floor, call 999, start CPR (compressions may dislodge object)

Infant Choking (under 1 year):
- 5 back blows (face-down along forearm)
- 5 chest thrusts (do NOT perform abdominal thrusts on infants)
- Alternate until clear or unconscious

BLEEDING

External Bleeding Control:
Step 1: Protect yourself with gloves or improvised barriers
Step 2: Apply direct pressure with clean cloth or dressing – FIRM and CONTINUOUS for at least 10 minutes; do not remove first dressing (add more on top if soaked)
Step 3: Elevate the injured part above the level of the heart (if no suspected fracture)
Step 4: Apply pressure bandage to maintain pressure
Step 5: Treat for shock: lay down, elevate legs (unless head/chest/spinal injury), keep warm

Tourniquets: Apply 5–7 cm above wound for life-threatening limb bleeding that cannot be controlled by direct pressure. Note the time of application. Commercial tourniquets (CAT, SOFTT-W) preferred; improvised must be wide (> 5 cm) and tight enough to stop bleeding.

Wound Packing: For deep wounds where direct pressure is insufficient (e.g., groin, axilla, neck): pack haemostatic dressings (e.g., QuikClot, CelOx) or clean cloth tightly into wound.

Internal Bleeding (suspected):
Signs: Pale, cold, clammy skin; rapid weak pulse; abdominal pain/rigidity; vomiting blood; blood in urine/faeces; swollen abdomen or thigh; coughing/vomiting blood.
Action: Call 999 immediately; keep warm; do not give food or drink; monitor consciousness and breathing.

BURNS

Burn Classification:
- Superficial (1st degree): Epidermis only; red, painful, no blisters (e.g., sunburn)
- Partial thickness (2nd degree): Epidermis and dermis; blisters, intense pain, moist red surface
- Full thickness (3rd degree): All skin layers; white/waxy/charred appearance; painless (nerve destruction)
- 4th degree: Into muscle, bone; requires surgical intervention

First Aid for Burns:
Step 1: Remove from heat source; ensure scene safety
Step 2: Cool the burn immediately with cool (15°C) running water for 20 minutes
  - Do NOT use ice, ice water, butter, toothpaste, or any household remedy
  - Do NOT remove stuck clothing; remove rings/watches near burn if possible
Step 3: Cover with clean non-fluffy material (cling film is ideal – do not wrap, lay loosely; or clean plastic bag for hands)
Step 4: Do NOT burst blisters (infection risk)
Step 5: Seek emergency care for:
  - Burns larger than 1% BSA (size of one palm) in adults
  - Any burn involving face, hands, feet, genitals, major joints, circumferential limb burns
  - Chemical or electrical burns
  - Inhalation injury (singed eyebrows/nasal hair, hoarse voice, stridor)
  - Burns in very young children or elderly patients
  - Full-thickness burns of any size

Chemical Burns: Brush off dry chemicals; irrigate with large volumes of water for 20–30 minutes; avoid contaminating eyes.
Electrical Burns: Ensure power is off before approaching; CPR may be needed; internal injury may be greater than external suggests.

FRACTURES

Signs and Symptoms of Fracture:
- Pain at site of injury
- Swelling, bruising, or deformity
- Tenderness to palpation
- Inability or unwillingness to use the affected limb
- Grating sensation (crepitus)
- Unnatural movement or position

Types: Open (compound) fracture – bone breaks through skin; Closed fracture – skin intact.

First Aid for Fractures:
Step 1: Do not attempt to straighten the fracture
Step 2: Immobilise the fracture in the position found:
  - Splint using whatever is available (rolled newspaper, padding, rigid material)
  - Splint should extend beyond joints above and below fracture
  - Check circulation, sensation, and movement before and after splinting (distal pulse, capillary refill)
Step 3: Apply ice pack wrapped in cloth (not directly on skin) to reduce swelling
Step 4: Elevate if possible (unless spinal injury)
Step 5: Seek medical attention; call 999 for femur, pelvis, or spinal fractures, or open fractures

Spinal Injury Precautions: If spinal injury is suspected (high-speed mechanism, neck/back pain, neurology), do NOT move the casualty unless in immediate danger of death. Support head and neck in neutral alignment and await emergency services.

SHOCK
Signs: Pale, cold, clammy skin; rapid weak pulse; rapid shallow breathing; confusion; thirst; dizziness.
First aid: Lay down; elevate legs unless head/chest/spinal injury; keep warm; call 999; do not give food/drink.
Types: Hypovolaemic (blood/fluid loss), anaphylactic (severe allergy), cardiogenic, neurogenic, septic.

ANAPHYLAXIS
Severe allergic reaction with rapid progression; life-threatening airway/breathing/circulation effects.
Recognition: Urticaria, angioedema, stridor, wheeze, hypotension, confusion after exposure to allergen.
Treatment: Adrenaline (epinephrine) IM 500 mcg (EpiPen/Jext auto-injector); call 999; second dose after 5 minutes if no improvement; antihistamines and steroids are secondary.
