CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE – RISK FACTORS, PREVENTION, STATINS, AND SURGERY

OVERVIEW
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of death globally, responsible for approximately 17.9 million deaths per year, representing 32% of all global deaths. CVD encompasses a group of disorders affecting the heart and blood vessels, including:
- Coronary artery disease (CAD) / ischaemic heart disease
- Cerebrovascular disease (stroke and TIA)
- Peripheral arterial disease (PAD)
- Heart failure
- Cardiomyopathies
- Arrhythmias
- Valvular heart disease
- Congenital heart defects
- Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism

CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE (CAD)
Atherosclerosis – the accumulation of lipid-laden plaques within the arterial wall – is the primary underlying mechanism. This process begins early in life and progresses silently over decades. Vulnerable plaques may rupture, triggering thrombosis and acute coronary syndromes (ACS): unstable angina, NSTEMI (Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction), or STEMI (ST-elevation myocardial infarction).

RISK FACTORS

Non-Modifiable Risk Factors:
- Age: Men ≥ 45 years; Women ≥ 55 years (post-menopause)
- Male sex (though women's risk equalises after menopause)
- Family history of premature CVD (first-degree relative, male < 55, female < 65 years)
- Ethnicity: Higher risk in South Asian, African-Caribbean populations

Modifiable Risk Factors:
- Hyperlipidaemia (elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL, elevated triglycerides)
- Hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥ 130 mmHg)
- Tobacco smoking (including e-cigarettes) – dose-dependent, significantly increases risk
- Diabetes mellitus (2–4× increased CVD risk)
- Obesity and overweight (especially central adiposity; waist circumference > 102 cm men, > 88 cm women)
- Physical inactivity
- Unhealthy diet (high saturated and trans fats, excess sodium, low fruit and vegetables)
- Excess alcohol consumption (> 14 units/week)
- Psychosocial stress (depression, social isolation, job stress – equivalent to 1–2 traditional risk factors)
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- Air pollution exposure

Emerging Risk Factors:
- Elevated Lp(a) lipoprotein – genetically determined; major independent risk factor
- Elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) – marker of systemic inflammation
- Atrial fibrillation (5× stroke risk)
- Clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP)
- Inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, lupus, HIV infection)
- Chronic kidney disease (eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²)
- Autoimmune disease
- Adverse pregnancy outcomes (pre-eclampsia, preterm delivery, gestational diabetes)

SYMPTOMS OF CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE
Stable angina: Chest pain or pressure (often described as squeezing, tightening, heaviness) brought on by exertion or stress; relieved within minutes by rest or sublingual nitrates.
Acute MI: Severe, crushing chest pain (may radiate to jaw, left arm, back, or epigastrium); diaphoresis; nausea; breathlessness; feeling of impending doom. NOTE: Women and diabetics may present atypically with fatigue, shortness of breath, jaw pain only, or nausea without chest pain.

PREVENTION

Primary Prevention (preventing first event):
- CVD risk calculation: QRISK3 (UK), Framingham (USA), SCORE2 (Europe)
- Target: 10-year CVD risk < 10% (low risk); 10–20% (moderate); > 20% (high)
- Smoking cessation (single most effective intervention)
- Blood pressure control (target < 130/80 mmHg)
- Statin therapy for high-risk individuals
- Diabetes management (HbA1c target < 53 mmol/mol)
- Weight management and regular exercise (150 min/week moderate aerobic + muscle strengthening)
- Healthy diet: Mediterranean-style or DASH diet
- Low-dose aspirin: No longer routinely recommended for primary prevention due to bleeding risk

Secondary Prevention (preventing recurrence):
- Dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + clopidogrel/ticagrelor/prasugrel) post-ACS
- High-intensity statin therapy (Atorvastatin 80 mg, Rosuvastatin 40 mg)
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs (after MI with reduced ejection fraction)
- Beta-blockers (post-MI, heart failure with reduced EF)
- SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists (CVD risk reduction in diabetics)
- Cardiac rehabilitation programme

STATIN THERAPY
Statins are HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors that reduce hepatic cholesterol synthesis, lowering LDL-C by 25–60%.

High-intensity statins (LDL-C reduction ≥ 50%): Atorvastatin 40–80 mg, Rosuvastatin 20–40 mg
Moderate-intensity statins (LDL-C reduction 30–50%): Atorvastatin 10–20 mg, Simvastatin 20–40 mg, Pravastatin 40–80 mg

Side effects:
- Myalgia (muscle aches) – most common; consider CK measurement
- Myopathy and rarely rhabdomyolysis (especially with drug interactions: cyclosporine, macrolides, antifungals, fibrates)
- Elevated liver enzymes (transaminases): Monitor LFTs at baseline
- New-onset diabetes mellitus (modest, 10–15% increased relative risk)
- Memory issues (rare, disputed by evidence)
Contraindications: Active liver disease, pregnancy, breastfeeding

Additional Lipid-Lowering Agents:
- Ezetimibe: Inhibits intestinal cholesterol absorption; add-on to statin or for statin intolerance; 20% additional LDL reduction
- PCSK9 inhibitors (Evolocumab, Alirocumab): Injectable monoclonal antibodies; 50–70% additional LDL reduction; for very high-risk patients or statin intolerance; expensive
- Inclisiran: siRNA injectable against PCSK9; given twice yearly
- Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa): Omega-3 fatty acid for elevated triglycerides with CVD risk reduction
- Fibrates: Primarily for hypertriglyceridaemia; gemfibrozil (avoid with statins), fenofibrate

CORONARY REVASCULARISATION

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) / Angioplasty:
- Balloon dilation of blocked coronary artery followed by stent placement
- Drug-eluting stents (DES) preferred; release antiproliferative drugs to prevent restenosis
- Post-procedure: Dual antiplatelet therapy for 12 months (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor)
- Preferred for STEMI, single or two vessel disease, symptomatic lesions

Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG):
- Surgical creation of alternate blood supply routes using internal mammary artery (gold standard) or saphenous vein grafts
- Preferred for left main coronary artery disease, triple vessel disease, diabetics with multivessel disease, reduced LV function
- Operative mortality: 1–3% for elective procedures; higher for emergency CABG
- Recovery: 6–12 weeks; long-term graft patency superior with arterial grafts

HEART FAILURE
Heart failure affects 64 million people globally. Key features: dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND, peripheral oedema, fatigue.
HFrEF (reduced ejection fraction < 40%): ACE inhibitor/ARB, beta-blocker, MRA (spironolactone), SGLT2 inhibitor (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin) – all reduce mortality.
HFpEF (preserved EF ≥ 50%): SGLT2 inhibitors reduce hospitalisation. Diuretics for symptom control.
